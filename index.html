How this tracker works (the method)
This follows the approach most prep providers recommend: roughly 300 hours of study, time weighted toward the heavy topics, practice questions straight after each chapter, and a final phase of full mock exams.
  1. Skim. Before a chapter, read the Learning Outcome Statements and the summary so you know what you'll be tested on.
  2. Study. Read the chapter properly and work through the examples. The schedule is built around this step.
  3. Practice Qs. Do the end-of-chapter or question-bank questions the same day or the next, and enter your score. 70%+ is on track, 60–69 needs a second look, and <60 is a weak area that gets priority in final review.
  4. Recall checks. 7 days after your practice Qs, and again 3 weeks later, a chapter shows up under Recall checks. Do 5–10 quick questions or explain the key formulas from memory. Spaced repetition like this is the cheapest way to stop early topics fading by February.
  5. Topic test. When a whole topic is finished, do 20–30 mixed questions on it and enter the score at the bottom of that topic.
  6. Final review & mocks. The last weeks before the exam are kept free for mixed review and 4 full timed mocks. Candidates who score 70%+ on mocks pass at a very high rate. In this phase, tick Review on each chapter as you revisit it. Weakest chapters come first.
Readiness is your scores weighted by each topic's share of the exam, so a weak Ethics score (15–20%) counts far more than a weak Derivatives one (5–8%). Use Re-plan remaining whenever you fall behind or get ahead: it spreads the unstudied chapters evenly up to the review phase.
